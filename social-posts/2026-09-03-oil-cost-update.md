Oil is trading above $95 a barrel this week after the U.S. and Iran exchanged fresh strikes near the Strait of Hormuz. Vessel crossings through the strait fell from 23 last Wednesday to just 10 on Monday, and Kuwait, Jordan, and Bahrain intercepted missiles overnight, per The National.

We keep a running tally of what this is costing the U.S. economy, comparing actual Brent prices to the government's own pre-war forecast of $58 a barrel. As of our latest read (Aug 25), Brent was already 52% above that forecast, and the added cost to U.S. petroleum spending had passed $148 billion since the war began in February — accumulating at roughly $620 million a day. Prices have moved higher since then, so both numbers keep climbing.
