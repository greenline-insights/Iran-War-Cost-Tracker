## Post draft

Oil is still running well above where the government expected it to be this year — and it's adding up.

As of this week, Brent crude is trading about 89% above the EIA's pre-war forecast for 2026. Tracked daily, that gap puts the added cost to the U.S. economy at roughly $160 billion since the war started — climbing by about $12,000 every second.

CNBC reported yesterday that the strain is showing up elsewhere too: the 10-year Treasury yield just hit its highest level since 2007, and Moody's Analytics puts the average household's added cost since the war began at about $1,760, more than half of it from energy alone.
