## Post text

Oil prices eased this week on reports that Iran and Oman are close to a deal on reopening shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Brent dropped below $88/barrel, its lowest point in about a month, as markets read the talks as a sign the war may not escalate further.

Worth keeping some context alongside that, though. Even with this week's pullback, oil is still running well above where it was expected to be before the war. The Iran War Oil Cost Tracker, which runs on official EIA price data, has Brent at $95.29/barrel as of its latest update (Aug 18) — 64% higher than the $58/barrel EIA had projected for all of 2026 back in February. EIA's daily figures post a few business days behind, so this week's dip hasn't shown up in the tracker yet.

Add that gap up across every day since the war began in February, and it comes to a bit over $143 billion in extra U.S. petroleum spending so far — still climbing by roughly $8,900 a second.
