## Post text

Oil jumped again Thursday night after reports that Iran struck targets in the Strait of Hormuz, even as Iran and Oman are reportedly close to some kind of arrangement on managing traffic through the strait. Brent and WTI both moved higher on the news.

It's a reminder of how little it takes to move this market right now. About a fifth of the world's oil passes through Hormuz, and any hint of fresh strikes there sends prices back up almost immediately, talks or no talks.

We've been tracking the economic cost of this day by day since the war started in late February, comparing actual oil prices against the U.S. government's own pre-war forecast. As of the latest EIA data (Aug 3), Brent is running near $88.90 a barrel — about 53% above the $58 baseline the EIA had penciled in before the war. The added cost to the U.S. economy over that stretch has now passed $132.9 billion, and it's still climbing at roughly $7,357 a second.
